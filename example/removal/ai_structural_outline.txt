- Sydney Opera House — among the most recognisable buildings in the world today
- Location: edge of Sydney Harbour, New South Wales, Australia
- Role: leading centre for the performing arts
- Distinctive shell-shaped roof; powerful symbol of both city and nation
- Millions of visitors each year, to admire the design
- Architect: Jørn Utzon, Danish; won international competition, 1957
- Construction began 1959; ran more than a decade; significant delays; rising costs
- Utzon resigned before completion
- Formal opening by Queen Elizabeth II, 1973; major milestone in Australian cultural history
- Wide range of performance spaces: Concert Hall, Joan Sutherland Theatre, Drama Theatre
- Thousands of performances yearly: opera, classical music, theatre, contemporary entertainment
- Several resident companies; rich and varied artistic programme
- Vital role in the cultural life of Australia
- Architecture widely regarded as a masterpiece of modern design
- Interlocking roof shells; ambitious engineering challenge for their time
- Innovative solutions required; methods influenced construction techniques worldwide
- Still studied by architects and engineers today
- 2007: added to UNESCO World Heritage List; outstanding value
- Testament to creative ambition and technical achievement
- Visitors from every corner of the globe; active venue for the arts
- Lasting symbol of Australian identity and cultural pride
